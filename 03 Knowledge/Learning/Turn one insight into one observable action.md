# Turn one insight into one observable action

> [!summary] Principle
> An insight becomes useful only when it changes a decision or produces visible behavior. Learning compounds through application, not collection.

## The idea

Collecting ideas creates the feeling of progress. Applying an idea creates evidence of progress. The smallest useful unit of learning is therefore not a saved insight, but an insight connected to a specific behavior.

An action is strong when it is:

- Observable: someone could tell whether it happened.
- Small: it can be completed without a new project.
- Timed: it has a clear moment or deadline.
- Reviewable: its result can inform the next decision.

## Action protocol

When an insight feels important, complete this sentence:

> Because I learned **[insight]**, I will **[observable action]** when/by **[time or trigger]**. I will know it worked when **[evidence]**.

Then:

1. Put the action in the relevant task or calendar system.
2. Link that action back to this note or its source.
3. Review the result after completion.
4. Keep, revise, or discard the principle based on evidence.

## Current application

- **Insight:** Position sizing protects decision quality.
- **Action:** Define the maximum acceptable loss before entering the next trade.
- **Trigger:** Before placing the order.
- **Evidence:** The risk amount is written in the decision journal before entry.
- **Review:** Did defining risk in advance change the decision or emotional response?

## Review questions

- What decision should this idea change?
- What is the smallest visible behavior that proves application?
- When will that behavior happen?
- What evidence will show whether it helped?
- Should this become a checklist, habit, rule, or project?

## When this is useful

- After reading, a course, or a mentoring session.
- When notes are accumulating but behavior is unchanged.
- When a principle sounds true but has not been tested.
- During weekly review, to turn highlights into commitments.

## Source context

Dr. Majed's mentor library is designed to “preserve the lesson, find the principle, apply the next action.” Its weekly practice is to turn one insight into one observable action, based on the idea that knowledge compounds only when it changes a decision.
